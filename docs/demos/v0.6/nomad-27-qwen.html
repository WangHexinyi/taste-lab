The Theme

Curatorial introduction
Edition 27 — 2027

“What remains between image, sound, body and space, once the signal has moved on?”

Porto Vela has always been a receiving city. Cranes on the horizon, antennae on the pier, radio bleeding over the breakwater in three languages and static. For seven days, NOMAD’s twenty-seventh edition settles into this coastline — into its turbine hall, its sea museum, its buried cistern and its public square — and listens back.

Between Signals is not a programme about technology. It is a programme about residue: the afterimage a projection leaves on a harbour wall; the low frequency a cistern holds long after the last chord; the choreography of a crowd crossing a square at dusk. Forty artists from nineteen countries work across four venues, each chosen for what its architecture already knows how to say.

Works drift between the four movements below. A film is heard before it is seen. A dance is a broadcast. A scaffold becomes an antenna. We ask you to move the way signals move — by day on foot between venues, by night on frequency.

Image

Projections, film programmes and video work that treat the screen as a membrane, not a window.

Sound

A twenty-four-channel cistern, a pier-length speaker array, and listening sessions at the waterline.

Body

Performance and dance that read the body as both receiver and transmitter.

Space

Architectural interventions that retune the city’s existing structures rather than add to them.

Featured — Sound movement

Mira Okonkwo-Lange

Tidal Memory

Lagos / Berlin · The Cistern · Days 2, 5, 7

Okonkwo-Lange records the infra-low of ports and tide stations and recomposes it as slow, architectural listening. For Porto Vela she suspends twenty-four channels at water level inside the Cistern, where the room’s nine-second reverberation becomes the final instrument.

03

Venues

Four structures, one coastline

Each venue keeps its own weather. The festival adds almost nothing — light, sound, seating, a door left open — and lets the buildings do what they have always done: hold, amplify, shelter and signal.

Character

1912 turbine hall, 18 m trusses, crane rails intact. The room answers sound with a long metallic tail.

Hosts

Latent Harbour · Ghost Bandwidth · Saltation · Carrier Wave night sets.

Access

Step-free from Doca Norte. Hearing loop at night sets. Capacity 1,800.

V2

Museu do Mar

Seaside museumScreening roomAuditorium

White galleries hung over the water. The museum carries the IMAGE movement: film and video programmes by day, talks in the auditorium, and a quiet room on level two for anyone who needs the festival to lower its voice.

Captioned screenings on Days 2 and 6. Capacity 420.

V3

The Cistern

Underground theatreSound art

Buried beneath the breakwater, forty-four columns standing in shallow water. The city’s old reservoir is now its most careful listening room: sessions are silent, seated, and limited to one hundred and twenty bodies.

Reached by a single stair of twenty-four steps; access companions admitted in addition to capacity.

About NOMAD

Curatorial statement & credits

NOMAD began in 2001 as a weekend of radio art and documentary film in a disused customs shed. It has returned every year since, never to the same building twice. The festival’s only fixed address is its method: arrive in a city, listen to what its structures already transmit, and answer with work made for those rooms and no others.

The twenty-seventh edition is the first to stay a full week in one place. Porto Vela earned the exception. A port that decommissioned its cranes but kept them standing, a museum built to face the weather, a reservoir buried under its own breakwater — the city is an archive of signals that never fully switched off.

We commissioned accordingly. Nothing here is a tour stop. Every installation is site-tuned, every performance rehearsed in the room it premieres in, every film programme cut against the grain of its screen — sea light on white walls, sodium light on harbour stone.

Between Signals is an invitation to attend to the interval: the space between transmission and reception where meaning is actually made. Bring good shoes. The frequencies are within walking distance of each other.

— Selma Andrade, Artistic Director
